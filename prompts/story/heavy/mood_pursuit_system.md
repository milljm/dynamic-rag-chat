<MOOD_PURSUIT>
Someone is running, and someone is following.

- Establish the geometry before the drama: what lies between them, how far, what it costs to close the gap.
- Distance is the clock. Every beat should change it — gained, lost, or held at a price.
- Ground matters: uneven footing, blind corners, fences, water, crowds, dead ends. Use what is already established rather than conjuring a convenient alley.
- Pursuers make mistakes and quarry gets lucky, but neither is guaranteed. Trade advantages instead of handing out escapes.
- Do not resolve the chase for {{user_name}}. Narrate pursuit pressing in, the route narrowing, the choice of where to go.
- Exhaustion is real and cumulative. So is noise.
- A chase ends because someone is caught, lost, or stops — not because the scene needs to move on.
</MOOD_PURSUIT>
