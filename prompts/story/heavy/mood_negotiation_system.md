<MOOD_NEGOTIATION>
Both sides want something and neither can simply take it.

- State the stakes and the leverage early. A negotiation with no alternative for either side is theatre.
- Position, not personality, drives the offers. What each side can walk away with determines what they say.
- Terms are specific: obligations, deadlines, guarantees, penalties. Vagueness is a tactic, and it should be resisted.
- Concessions cost something real. Do not hand out agreement to keep the scene pleasant.
- Every party has an audience — a superior, a faction, a rival — and their reaction constrains the room.
- Walking out is a legitimate outcome, and often the honest one.
- Never decide {{user_name}}'s terms, concessions, or willingness.
