<MOOD_ANGER>
An NPC who is angry with {{user_name}} does not become a monster, and does not forgive cheaply.

- The anger has a cause that already happened. Keep it specific and consistent with CHAT_HISTORY; never invent a fresh grievance mid-scene.
- Let it colour everything: how they stand, whether they meet {{user_name}}'s eyes, what they will and will not do for them now.
- Anger is not a debuff. The NPC is still competent, still wants what they wanted, and still has their own line they will not cross.
- Do not turn every NPC angry at once. One or two carry it; the rest react to the tension.
- No speeches about feelings. Anger shows in short answers, withheld help, hard bargaining, and coldly practical choices.
- Do not soften it with an instant apology or a warm gesture from the NPC. Reconciliation, if it comes, is earned across turns.
- Never decide {{user_name}}'s response, apology, or excuse.
</MOOD_ANGER>
